What actually goes wrong

Ranked by what it does to your wallet, not by how often it is mentioned online.

MAJOR

High-pressure fuel pump — sudden stall, no warning

The single largest complaint category on this car. Owners describe abrupt power loss and stalling in traffic, with the whole high-pressure fuel system replaced. One filing documents two separate failures, at 39,066 and 57,376 miles.

NHTSA

16 fuel and propulsion complaints on the 2017 X5 — more than any other category.

OUR TAKE

This is the one to actually worry about, and it's not what you'll read on the forums. Losing power in a live traffic lane is a different category of problem from a gasket weeping oil.

Cooling system — water pump, fan, overheating

Oil leaks — real, but forum-grade not federal-grade

Valve cover and filter housing gaskets harden with heat and this engine is the age where both give up. Consistently reported by owners — though notably it barely registers in federal complaint data, because a slow leak isn't a safety issue.

Take this to the inspection

Ask directly whether the high-pressure fuel pump has been replaced. It's the top complaint category and owners report failures well under 60,000 miles. This one can strand you.

Use a BMW specialist, not the shop down the road. $200 here is the best money in this entire transaction.

Ask for water pump and thermostat records. No paperwork at 89,000 miles means assume it's due and price it in.

Answer this honestly before you go: could you absorb a surprise $2,500 twice in the first year? If not, walk. There's no price that fixes this.
